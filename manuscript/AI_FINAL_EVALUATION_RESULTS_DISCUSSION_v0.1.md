# Results and Discussion — Model 0 Final Evaluation v0.1

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Evaluation design and interpretation frame

The final evaluation compares the three information architectures, N, S, and F, under two decision architectures: the RuleBased benchmark and learned AI policies. The frozen evaluation contains 3,600 held-out trajectories across 200 common scenarios. The primary analysis focuses on five preregistered outcomes and the two interaction estimands, Gamma_V for the N-to-S transition and Gamma_H for the S-to-F transition. The interaction design asks whether a change in information architecture has a different operational effect when decisions are generated by learned AI policies rather than by the RuleBased benchmark.

The AI results must be interpreted as outcomes from **learned policies under the pre-registered finite training budget**. Only 1 of the 15 AI training runs met the preregistered training-stability criterion; the remaining 14 did not stabilize under that budget. All 15 frozen policies are nevertheless retained in the evaluation. No training seed is excluded, reweighted, retrained, or replaced after observing held-out performance. [P4]

### 4.2 PRIMARY results: N-to-S interaction

The clearest primary N-to-S result occurs for the mean absolute target-allocation gap. The frozen Gamma_V estimate is -18.367599, with a frozen 95% bootstrap interval of [-49.124749, -1.245426]. Thus, relative to the RuleBased benchmark, the N-to-S transition is associated with a larger reduction in the target-allocation gap under the learned AI policies. [P1]

This pattern is specific to the direct allocation metric rather than a general improvement across all primary outcomes. The service-level Gamma_V estimate is -0.136466 with an interval of [-0.283873, 0.007512], while the corresponding waste-share, importer-procurement-bullwhip, and mean-total-inventory intervals also span zero. These results do not support a universal claim that moving from N to S improves downstream performance under AI. Instead, the primary evidence is more consistent with an information-by-decision-architecture interaction that is strongest in the direct coordination outcome. [P3]

### 4.3 PRIMARY results: S-to-F interaction

For the S-to-F transition, the clearest primary result appears in service level. The frozen Gamma_H estimate is 0.150154, with a frozen 95% bootstrap interval of [0.004007, 0.361032]. Relative to the RuleBased benchmark, the S-to-F transition is therefore associated with a larger service-level increase under the learned AI policies. [P2]

The other primary Gamma_H intervals span zero. In particular, importer procurement bullwhip has a large positive point estimate of 80.501856 but a wide interval of [-11.242796, 160.210667], and its between-seed dispersion is also large. Mean total inventory, waste share, and target-allocation gap likewise do not provide a common directional pattern for the S-to-F transition. The primary evidence therefore does not imply that full transparency dominates selective transparency across operational dimensions. [P3]

### 4.4 PRIMARY seed heterogeneity and finite-budget learning

The pooled interaction estimates conceal substantial variation across the five frozen training seeds for several downstream outcomes. For example, seed-specific Gamma_H values for importer procurement bullwhip range from -75.774410 to 218.966532. Gamma_V values for mean total inventory range from -77.012547 to 25.504228. This cross-seed spread is part of the frozen result rather than a nuisance to be removed by post-hoc selection. [P3]

Together with the training-stability audit, this heterogeneity places an important boundary on interpretation. The experiment evaluates a fixed, preregistered learning budget rather than a set of policies demonstrated to have converged. The results therefore characterize the behavior of the frozen learned policies under that finite budget; they should not be read as estimates of an asymptotic or optimal AI policy. [P4]

### 4.5 EXPLORATORY supporting results

The secondary outcomes are reported as **EXPLORATORY** and do not alter the primary interpretation above. For the N-to-S transition, the exploratory stock-allocation-gap Gamma_V estimate is -18.904744 with an exploratory 95% interval of [-49.059334, -2.075621]. This pattern is directionally consistent with the primary target-allocation-gap result, but it remains secondary evidence and is not promoted to a confirmatory finding. [E1]

For the S-to-F transition, the exploratory total-lost-sales Gamma_H estimate is -3604.440665 with an exploratory 95% interval of [-8664.310721, -96.279457]. Retail-order bullwhip, total waste, and the stock-allocation-gap Gamma_H have exploratory intervals that span zero. These results may help diagnose downstream operating consequences, but they do not replace the R1 primary narrative. [E2]

The manuscript-facing interaction estimates are reported in the frozen PRIMARY and EXPLORATORY tables and figures in `outputs/manuscript_v0.1/`. Those displays reproduce the frozen registries and do not introduce new statistical calculations. [R3]

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Information architecture and decision architecture interact

The central result is not a universal ordering of N, S, and F, nor a universal advantage of AI over RuleBased decisions. Instead, the operational effect of changing information architecture depends on the decision architecture that consumes the information. [P5]

This distinction matters because transparency changes the information available to a decision process, but the operational consequence of that information depends on how the process maps observations into actions. In Model 0, the N-to-S transition is most clearly reflected in the primary target-allocation-gap interaction, whereas the S-to-F transition is most clearly reflected in the primary service-level interaction. The absence of a common directional pattern across the remaining primary outcomes cautions against treating transparency as a scalar intervention for which “more” must always be operationally better. [P1] [P2] [P3]

### 5.2 Direct coordination effects versus downstream outcomes

The result pattern also distinguishes direct coordination measures from more aggregated downstream outcomes. The primary target-allocation-gap result is comparatively clear under N-to-S, while waste, procurement bullwhip, and inventory show wider intervals and stronger seed heterogeneity. Exploratory stock-allocation-gap evidence points in a similar direction, but its role is supportive rather than confirmatory. [P1] [P3] [E1]

One interpretation consistent with the frozen evidence is that changing information architecture can first alter how decisions coordinate allocation targets, while the eventual effects on inventory, waste, service, and bullwhip depend on additional propagation through the simulated supply chain. The present experiment does not establish that this mechanism is universal; it shows that direct and downstream metrics should not be treated as interchangeable indicators of transparency value within the Model 0 environment. [P5]

### 5.3 Selective versus full transparency

The N-to-S and S-to-F results suggest different operational margins. Under N-to-S, the strongest primary interaction is a reduction in the target-allocation gap. Under S-to-F, the strongest primary interaction is an increase in service level. At the same time, the large and uncertain procurement-bullwhip response under S-to-F illustrates that an information architecture can have different implications across performance dimensions. [P1] [P2] [P3]

Accordingly, the results do not support a blanket recommendation to maximize transparency. A buyer or platform evaluating information disclosure together with AI-enabled decision support should consider which operational margin the additional information is intended to improve and which downstream responses may become more variable. This is a conditional design implication from the simulation, not a claim that one architecture is universally preferred. [M1]

### 5.4 Managerial implications for AI-enabled information systems

Three implementation implications follow from the frozen results.

First, information design and decision design should be evaluated jointly. An information architecture that changes outcomes under one decision architecture need not produce the same response under another. [M1]

Second, organizations should monitor direct coordination metrics and downstream operating metrics separately. Improvements in target or stock allocation alignment do not automatically imply lower waste, lower bullwhip, or lower inventory. [M2]

Third, learning stability is an implementation concern. Because 14 of the 15 training runs did not satisfy the preregistered stability criterion within the fixed budget, operational deployment should not assume that a trained policy has reached a stable endpoint simply because a training budget has been exhausted. In this study, retaining all frozen seeds rather than selecting the most favorable run is essential to preserving the preregistered design. [P4] [M3]

### 5.5 Limitations and interpretation boundary

Several limitations define the scope of these findings. The analysis is based on the frozen Model 0 simulation environment, not field data. The AI policies are finite-budget learned policies rather than policies established to be converged or optimal. The primary uncertainty layer reports bootstrap intervals without p-values or formal significance claims. Several downstream outcomes display large between-seed dispersion, so pooled averages should be interpreted together with heterogeneity. Finally, the exploratory secondary outcomes are supporting diagnostics and cannot be used to retrofit the confirmatory primary narrative. [L1]

These boundaries also define the next research steps. Claims of robustness across alternative demand, perishability, availability, or network conditions would require a separately preregistered simulation gate. Likewise, a different training budget, checkpoint-selection rule, or inferential method would constitute a new scientific analysis rather than an extension of the frozen R4 manuscript layer. [L2]
